# Climate window - derived, not assumed

Generated by `scripts/derive_climate_window.py` on 2026-09-09.

The climate feature is autumn rainfall over the fruiting season. This file
fixes which days and which years that means, derived from the records
themselves, before any model is fitted.

### The dates carry biology, not only the walking calendar

The three medians span 30 days, from Cantharellus cibarius on 28 Aug to Craterellus tubaeformis on 27 Sep.
The target group as a whole - which is the recording-effort calendar,
i.e. when people go looking at all - peaks on 8 Sep.
The species medians sit 11-19 days off that peak,
in both directions. If the record dates were purely a function of when
people walk, the three would cluster on the effort peak instead. They
do not, which is a small piece of evidence that the dates carry real
phenology. It says nothing about whether the *locations* do - that is
what the blocked-CV result is for.

## The window

**Day 221 (9 Aug) to day 293 (20 Oct)**, the mean
of the three species' 10th and 90th day-of-year
percentiles.

Each species gets one vote. Pooling all 1,553 records instead would give
day 211 (30 Jul) to day 290 (17 Oct), a window set mainly by whichever species was
recorded most - and CLAUDE.md names *A. muscaria* as over-recorded
relative to its actual distribution. Letting record counts choose the
window would import the exact effort bias the target-group background
exists to remove. The difference between the two is small, which is the
expected result and not a reason to skip the distinction.

One window is used for all three species. They demonstrably do not fruit
at the same time, but CLAUDE.md requires the same feature stack across
species: the ordering test compares the three against each other, and
three different climate features would make those three numbers
incomparable. The cost of a shared window is stated here rather than
hidden - it fits the middle species best and the tails worst.

## The years

Mean over **1991-2020**, the WMO standard normal period,
chosen because it is the standard rather than because it suits the data.
The feature is therefore a climatology: *is this place reliably wet in
autumn*, not *was it wet the week someone walked past*. Year-matched
weather would be a conditions layer, which CLAUDE.md places in Phase 2.

## Feature definition, registered

```
autumn_rain = mean over years 1991..2020 of
              sum of seNorge_2018 rr over day 221..293 (9 Aug-20 Oct)
              at 1 km, resampled to the 32 m terrain grid
```

### Known weaknesses of this feature, stated before it is fitted

* seNorge's effective resolution is coarser than its 1 km grid, and
  quality tracks station density. Coastal Agder is better instrumented
  than the inland valleys.
* Autumn rainfall over Agder is dominated by a coast-inland and
  orographic gradient. **Population is also coastal.** This feature can
  therefore stand in for accessibility, which is exactly the failure the
  target-group background exists to prevent. Its permutation importance
  must be reported separately, and a model that leans on it while the
  terrain features do nothing should be read as suspicious, not as a
  result.
* At climatological scale the exact window matters far less than it
  would for a daily layer: the spatial pattern of mean autumn rainfall
  is similar across neighbouring windows. That is testable - the
  correlation between this window and shifted variants is reported once
  the raster exists, as a sensitivity check rather than an assumption.
