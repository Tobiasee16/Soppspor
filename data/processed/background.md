# Target-group background

Generated by `scripts/report_background.py`.

Background points are drawn from the locations of **all** fungal records in
the region, not scattered uniformly. A record of any fungus means somebody
stood there, looked, and wrote something down. Using those places as
background gives presences and background the same observer bias, so it
cancels instead of being learned. Hard constraint 3.

## 1. What the pool is

- Source: Artskart `observations/list`, `filter.taxongroups=9` (Sopper)
  and `filter.countys=42` (Agder). Server-side filtering, verified to
  change TotalCount and cross-checked against our own polygon clip.
- Reported by the API: **82,129** records; downloaded 82,129.

| Stage | Records | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Downloaded rows | 82,129 | one row per record per admin unit |
| Usable coordinates, deduplicated | 82,129 | drops rows with no East/North |
| Inside the Agder fylke polygon | 82,129 | matches the server-side county filter exactly |
| Stated precision <= 250 m | 69,391 | not assuming unstated precision is good |
| Thinned to 1000 m | 3,882 | **the background pool** |

Thinning removes 94% of the records, and it should: a
heavily visited forest can carry hundreds of records, and unthinned it
would contribute hundreds of background points and drown the rest of
Agder. That the reduction is this steep is itself a finding - the records
are packed into a small number of places.

The pool is 6% of the precision-filtered
records, and it supplies both the background and the null control.

The three focal species are 1,553 of 82,129 rows (1.9%). They are deliberately left in: target-group
background is the set of places the group was recorded, and removing the
focal species from it would make background systematically *less* likely to
sit where the species occurs, which is a bias in the opposite direction.

## 2. Is the background actually non-uniform?

The Agder boundary polygon spans 23,055 square-kilometre cells.
Fungal records touch **3,866** of them - 17% of the region.

**About 83% of Agder has never had a single fungus recorded on
it.** Uniform background would put most of its points on that untouched
ground, and a model could then score well knowing nothing whatsoever
except which ground people visit. Section 3 measures how large that
effect actually is.

(The cell count follows the administrative polygon, which includes water
inside the boundary; Agder's land area is nearer 16,400 km2. The coverage
fraction is therefore pessimistic rather than flattering.)

## 3. How much apparent signal is just accessibility?

A model given **nothing but coordinates** is run twice per species: once
against uniformly scattered background, once against target-group
background. It has no environmental data at all, so anything it scores is
knowledge of where records are.

Blocked CV, 7500 m blocks, 5 folds, 4000 m exclusion
buffer, background capped at 2,000 points for run time.

| Species | vs uniform background | vs target-group background | corrected |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Craterellus tubaeformis* | 0.805 | 0.584 | +0.221 |
| *Cantharellus cibarius* | 0.822 | 0.592 | +0.230 |
| *Amanita muscaria* | 0.790 | 0.595 | +0.195 |

The first column is the number we would have reported had we used uniform
background and no environmental data whatsoever. The third column is how
much of that was accessibility rather than anything about fungi.

## 4. Null control on real geography

300 null presences and 2000 background points drawn disjointly
from the same pool (0 coordinates shared - must be 0).
The null carries sampling effort and no ecology at all, so a correct
pipeline returns 0.5.

**Blocked-CV AUC for the synthetic null: 0.526** (+/- 0.071 across folds) - PASS

The null sits where it should, but note it is not exactly 0.5, and the
excess is not noise to wave away. It is what
[autocorrelation_range.md](autocorrelation_range.md) predicts: at a
4000 m buffer a third to a half of the short-range spatial
dependence survives, so a small positive bias in the null is the
expected consequence rather than a surprise.

**Treat 0.53 as the noise floor of this pipeline.** A species
scoring near it has demonstrated nothing, however far above 0.5 it
looks. The fold spread is wide too, so the floor is a band and not a
line.

This is a necessary condition for reading any real result, not a
sufficient one. It is measured with coordinates as the only feature;
it must be re-run against the real feature stack once that exists,
because environmental variables correlate with accessibility in ways
coordinates alone do not capture.

## Carried forward

- The pool is built from presences of the whole group, so it inherits any
  bias shared across all fungal recording - a bias toward autumn, toward
  conspicuous fruiting bodies, toward southern Agder. Target-group
  background corrects for *where people looked*, not for what they were
  willing to write down.
- Records with no stated precision are dropped rather than assumed good,
  which discards real observations. The pool is large enough to afford it.
- The accessibility probe uses coordinates only. It bounds the effort
  signal; it does not tell us how much survives once real environmental
  features are present, because those correlate with accessibility too.
