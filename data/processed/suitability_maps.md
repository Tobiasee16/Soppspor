# Suitability maps - Kristiansand, 16 m

Generated by `scripts/build_maps.py` on 2026-09-12.

Phase 0 artefact 3. 4029 x 2823 at 16 m, EPSG:25833, model `maxent-style (L1 logistic, linear + quadratic)`.

**Fitted on Agder fylke minus Kristiansand kommune (post-2020 merge) and a 4000 m buffer.** These are predictions onto ground the model never saw, not a fit redrawn over its own training data.

**These maps are not an edibility assessment and not a species identification.** They shade terrain by habitat plausibility from public geodata. A high cell means the terrain resembles places where the species has been recorded. It does not mean anything is there.

The sensitive set comes from Miljodirektoratet Naturbase NiN naturtyper - see [naturbase.md](naturbase.md) for the pull and for the rule deciding what counts as sensitive. The mode is `blur`, not `mask`, because a hole in a map is itself a signal: a sharp nodata disc says 'something is here' as loudly as a high score would.

**This is not a security control.** Naturbase is public, so a determined reader can recover these locations from the source. Suppression removes the fine structure that would let someone walk to a specific stand; it raises the effort, it does not prevent.

**It covers only what has been surveyed.** Most of Agder has never been mapped for naturtyper, so these polygons mark where somebody looked, not where the sensitive habitat is. The unmapped ground is the larger gap and suppression cannot close it.

## What the colours mean, and what they do not

Single-hue sequential ramp, blue, light to dark. CLAUDE.md hard constraint 7 forbids red-to-green anywhere in this project: red/green reads as danger/safe, which is the wrong signal for a mushroom product. The ramp is stretched between the 2nd and 98th percentile of each map's own scores, so **colours are not comparable between species.** A dark cell on the *A. muscaria* map and a dark cell on the *C. tubaeformis* map do not represent the same score.

The values are relative habitat plausibility, not probability of occurrence and not yield. They are uncalibrated model scores.

## The null map is the control, and it needs reading carefully

`SYNTHETIC - NOT REAL DATA` is mapped on the same grid, from presences drawn from the sampling-effort surface with no ecology in them at all.

**It shows terrain structure too, and that is not the scandal it looks like.** Every map here is stretched between its own 2nd and 98th percentile, so any variation at all is expanded to fill the whole ramp. The null has a transfer AUC of 0.486 - no discriminative power - but its fitted coefficients are not zero, so it still paints valleys and ridges, and the stretch then makes that look as emphatic as a real signal.

What separates them is the *range*, which is why the table above reports it:

* Craterellus tubaeformis: max 0.686, 1.8x the null's 0.373
* Cantharellus cibarius: max 0.633, 1.7x the null's 0.373
* Amanita muscaria: max 0.503, 1.4x the null's 0.373
* Boletus edulis: max 0.669, 1.8x the null's 0.373
* Craterellus cornucopioides: max 0.665, 1.8x the null's 0.373
* Leccinum versipelle: max 0.979, 2.6x the null's 0.373
* Suillus luteus: max 0.335, 0.9x the null's 0.373
* Hydnum repandum: max 0.527, 1.4x the null's 0.373

The species models spread their scores over a genuinely wider range than the null does; the null sits in a narrow band that the colour stretch inflates. So compare *ranges* between these maps, never colours - the ramps are not on a common scale.

The honest summary: the shapes on the species maps are largely terrain, because terrain is what these features mostly encode. That was the reading in FINDINGS section 7 and nothing here overturns it.

## Known limits carried into this artefact

* Terrain is 32 m resampled to the 16 m grid, not recomputed at 16 m. TWI at 16 m is a different variable and the model was not fitted on it. The 16 m detail in these maps comes from SR16 and the substrate layer.
* SR16 is a model, not ground truth: tree species correct in roughly two-thirds of pixels, volume error 30-60% of the mean.
* These maps are fitted on the transfer split, so the accuracy behind them is the **transferability** figure, not the blocked-CV one: 0.605-0.730 AUC, with the null at 0.486. See [transferability.md](transferability.md). That is weak-to-moderate. A map drawn from it will be wrong in specific places and should be read as a hypothesis about terrain, not a prediction about mushrooms.
* The registered species ordering does **not** hold on this split. *C. tubaeformis*, predicted to be the most predictable of the three, scores lowest here. Do not read the tubaeformis map as the most trustworthy of the set.
* The Boyce index asks whether predicted suitability rises monotonically with observed presence density, which is the question that matters for a *surface* rather than for a ranking. On the corrected quantile-window implementation it is +0.70 to +0.92 for the three species and about zero for the null, so these surfaces are coherent even where they are not accurate. The published equal-width implementation reports the opposite and is biased; see [boyce_diagnosis.md](boyce_diagnosis.md).
