# Basemap tiles for the interface

Generated by `scripts/fetch_basemap.py`. Not a model input; nothing here reaches a feature stack.

## Why the basemap is embedded rather than tiled from a server

A published artifact runs under a content policy that blocks every external image and network request. A normal slippy map against a tile server is therefore not possible at all - not slow, not degraded, blocked. The basemap ships inside the page as fixed tiles instead.

## No aerial imagery

`wms.geonorge.no/skwms1/wms.nib` (Norge i bilder) answers an anonymous request with `Bruker kan ikke autentiseres` - it needs a Kartverket account. That is a credential, not a missing route, the same class of blocker as FKB-AR5.

## Layers

| key | service | resolution | tile | quality | size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `plain` | `norges_grunnkart` / `Norges_grunnkart` | 9.0 m/px | 1255x1304 px | q76 | 4.00 MB |
| `topo` | `topo` / `topo` | 12.0 m/px | 941x978 px | q70 | 3.79 MB |

`trails` is not a background at all but a transparent overlay of marked routes from Turrutebasen, drawn over whichever background is showing. The zones say where to look and nothing about how to get there, and a zone you cannot walk into is decoration. It is cheap to carry because the sheet is about 99 % transparent and quantises to eight colours.

`plain` is the default and is for orientation: pale blue water, muted green forest, warm buff settlement, place names and roads, no contour clutter. `topo` is the secondary layer and is for reading the ground itself - contours, marsh, tracks, vegetation boundaries - which is what you want once you are standing in it.

Topo is carried coarser, and that is measured rather than assumed. At a matched 9 m/px and *lower* JPEG quality it still came out 40 % larger than plain (5.57 MB against 3.99 MB): contour lines are high-frequency detail across the whole sheet and JPEG cannot compress them. Both layers at 9 m/px reached 15.8 MB once base64-encoded, against a 16 MB page ceiling.

`norges_grunnkart_graatone` was the default in an earlier build and has been dropped: it is calm, but greyscale means water and land are both pale grey, which removes the strongest cue for where you are.

## Extent

Taken from **modelled cells in payload.js**: the bounding box of cells the model actually scores, plus 3000 m to pan into. Kristiansand's official boundary runs far out into the Skagerrak, so the kommune bounding box is 45 x 64 km of which roughly 40 % is open sea, and only 22 % of the model grid holds a scored cell. Earlier sheets were fetched over that bbox and spent most of their pixels on empty water.

| | |
|---|---|
| extent (EPSG:25833) | 59528, 6449320, 104696, 6496280 |
| span | 45.2 x 47.0 km |
| grid | 4 x 4 tiles per layer |
| total | 7.79 MB across 2 layers |

The interface draws only the tiles that intersect the viewport, and only the layer on screen is ever decoded - the other one stays an unfetched data URI until you switch to it, which keeps a phone from holding both sheets in memory at once. The deepest zoom is 5 m/px, so plain upscales by under 2x; the single sheet this replaced was 31.7 m/px against the same zoom, which is why zooming in produced a blur.
