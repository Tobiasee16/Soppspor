# Spatial dependence in the occurrence records, and what it means for the buffer

Generated by `scripts/derive_buffer.py`. No model fitted.

**Headline: there is no autocorrelation range in these records to set the
buffer from.** Spatial dependence declines smoothly across every distance
we can measure and never reaches a floor. The buffer is therefore a
trade-off between leakage and training data, chosen and declared, not a
threshold derived from the data. Both sides are quantified below.

All figures: Agder fylke, records thinned to 1000 m and
filtered to stated precision <= 250 m - the exact set blocked CV will
run on. g(r) in 1000 m bins out to 30 km (39 simulations).

The prediction extent is not analysed here. It is held out and evaluated
once rather than cross-validated, so no exclusion buffer applies to it.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Craterellus tubaeformis* | 248 | 9.9 | 3.5 | 2.1 | 1.87 | **no** |
| *Cantharellus cibarius* | 396 | 7.5 | 3.5 | 2.1 | 1.88 | **no** |
| *Amanita muscaria* | 260 | 6.8 | 3.2 | 1.9 | 1.62 | **no** |

A far-field g near 1.9 means that at 25-30 km apart, two records are still
almost twice as likely to co-occur as chance. That is the regional
concentration of recording effort - more records near Kristiansand and
Arendal than in the inland valleys - and it is not what an exclusion buffer
can or should fix. Hard constraint 3 (target-group background sampling) is
the instrument for it.

## 2. Against the records' own regional density: still no floor

Every species, at every kernel width, is still declining at the outer edge
of what can be estimated. The estimator refuses to return a range rather
than reporting the level it happens to have reached, which would be an
artefact of where we stopped measuring.

The result is insensitive to the kernel width, which is the main thing that
could have driven it. It is a property of the records, not of the method.

## 3. What each buffer buys and what it costs

Excess remaining: the share of short-range clumping still present at that
separation, against the 30 km kernel null. Since the curve
never flattens, the far-field level used is an upper bound on the true one,
so these are floors on the leakage, not estimates of it.

## 4. The choice, and what it does not fix

**buffer_m = 4000.** Chosen as the largest buffer that still leaves
85% of training presences in the worst species, because with no
range in the data the binding constraint is the data budget rather than any
property of the point pattern. It is a declared trade-off. It is not the
autocorrelation range, and it must not be described as one.

At this buffer at least **48% of the short-range spatial
dependence remains** in the worst species. Blocked CV on this data is
therefore still optimistic. Three consequences follow, and they are not
optional:

1. **The null control becomes the load-bearing check, not the buffer.** Hard
   constraint 5 already requires the synthetic null in every run. Given
   residual leakage of this size, a null that scores above 0.5 is the
   expected failure, and it is the number to read first.
2. **The blocked-versus-random gap must be reported for every species.** The
   absolute blocked figure carries known contamination; the gap between the
   two schemes is the more honest quantity and the harness returns both.
3. **A blocked AUC modestly above 0.5 is not evidence of ecology.** The
   predicted ordering across the three species, and the null sitting at
   0.5, remain the falsification test. A single species' number does not.

### What would settle this properly

Leakage can be measured directly rather than bounded, but not yet: it needs
target-group background, which does not exist. With it, the synthetic null
species can be run at a sweep of buffers, and the buffer chosen where the
null returns to 0.5 on real geography. That is a measurement of the thing
itself instead of a proxy for it, and it should replace this choice.
